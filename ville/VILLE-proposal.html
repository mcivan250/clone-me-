The idea

There are so many people here doing interesting things. They're just never in the same room.

Making music. Taking photographs. Shooting films. Designing clothes. Building businesses. Painting. Dancing. Starting things. All of it happening at once, across a city, in separate rooms.

VILLE puts them in one. Not to network, not to make connections — just to be there, hear something new, meet someone, and have a night worth talking about.

And every time, we change the room.

The name

A ville is a town. VILLE is a town that moves.

It has no address. It has a room, and the room changes.

You don't live in it. You turn up. That is the entire membership requirement, and it's why the card matters — it's the only record of who was there.

Kampala is where it starts. The town is what travels.

The rule

Same room. Same ticket.

No VIP. No tables. No sections. Nobody gets a better version of the night than anybody else.

This is the decision everything else protects. The moment VILLE has a rope it becomes every other event in the city, and the reason people came stops being true. It applies to partners too — no branded VIP, no separated area. Saying so out loud is part of what makes the offer credible.

Same ticket applies within a chapter. Chapters are priced to their room: a student courtyard and a December finale are not the same night and shouldn't cost the same.

Bring your own. Corkage is charged per cooler, not per person. A group of six splits 100,000 and pays under 20,000 each — arriving together becomes the natural move, which is exactly the behaviour the room wants.

The card

Everyone who comes gets one. It's marked at every chapter you attend.

Four marks means you were there for the whole first season. Not a tier, not access, nothing anyone can buy their way into — only something you can have been present for.

It costs almost nothing to produce, and it's the only reason a person needs to think about Chapter Three while standing in Chapter Two.

The roster

Twelve people are cast before any campaign is written.

The usual order is concept, then budget, then find creators to amplify it. VILLE runs it backwards. We find the twelve people this audience already listens to, and the campaign comes out of them.

One creator is a coin flip. Twelve is a channel we own for six weeks — a photographer, a DJ, a filmmaker, a designer, a fashion creative, an entrepreneur, a student, and the people who simply know everyone. They are not amplification bought after the fact. They are the evidence the room exists.

The launch

Eight weeks. Three phases. No ticket on sale until the third.

Speed is the story. VILLE didn't spend a year announcing itself. It appeared.

Phase one — Who's in the room?

We don't announce an event. We show people. Short films from the roster, no long interviews, no corporate questions. Who are you? What are you into? What are you making? Who are you looking for?

The photographer is here. The DJ is here. The designer is here. The founder is here. The student is here. You're here too.

Phase two — The idea

Only after the faces:

We spend so much time connected to people online. Maybe it's time we got in the same room.

Phase three — Off the grid

Then we tease places, not lineups. A warehouse door. A staircase. A courtyard wall. A hill at dusk. No explanation — just that VILLE is going off the grid. Then the date lands.

Chapter One falls on 31 October. Every venue in Kampala will run a themed night. VILLE will not — no costumes, no theme. A themed launch would make this a novelty instead of a beginning. So on the one night of the year the whole city dresses as someone else:

Come as you are.

The ladder

Spend climbs as the property earns proof. Chapter One is a rave in a raw warehouse: production goes to sound and light, not decor, and there is no headliner fee. Making Design Hub look expensive would damage the brand.

Chapter One sits at 15,000,000 deliberately. Three hundred tickets at 50,000 is 15,000,000. Tickets alone cover the night.

The gate — 10 October

Chapter One must be able to run unsponsored. Partner income is upside, not survival. That is what lets GENrise walk into every partner conversation able to say the season happens either way — the only posture that produces a good deal, for both sides.

If no Season Partner is signed by 10 October, Chapter One runs on the reduced budget and partner conversations move to Chapter Two.

Chapter Two

The lowest ticket of the season sits in the highest-value room. A courtyard of UCU students is the most commercially attractive audience VILLE will assemble all year — which is why Chapter Two carries the largest partner target while charging 25,000 at the door.

Why now

VILLE runs 31 October to 12 December — the exact window Q4 budgets are built for. This isn't a request for new money. It's the cultural play that starts before everyone else's December activation and runs straight through it, in front of 1,200 people across four spaces in six weeks.

What partners get that they can't buy elsewhere: participation in something a specific, hard-to-reach audience already cares about, content made by the people in the room rather than an agency outside it, and a report a customer could verify.
