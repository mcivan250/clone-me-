# VILLE — Season One Runbook

Internal. Not for partners.
GENrise · Kampala · September – December 2026

---

## The operating principle

Season One has one job: produce proof. Not revenue, not scale, not a 2027 platform. Proof — an archive, a roster, a list of names, a set of passports in circulation, and four chapters that happened on the dates they said they would.

Every decision this season gets tested against one question: **does this make the evidence stronger?** If it only makes the night bigger, it's optional.

---

## Four workstreams

Each needs one owner. One person can hold two, nobody should hold three.

### 01 — Roster and content
Cast twelve. Produce their films. Run the channel.

The roster is cast before the campaign is written. Selection is by who listens to them, not by follower count — a photographer with 4,000 real peers beats a page with 80,000 strangers. Mix disciplines deliberately: no more than two from any one field.

The twelve carry across all four chapters and into Season Two. They are not a Chapter One campaign.

**Owns:** casting, film production, publishing schedule, the season film.

### 02 — Production and spaces
Three confirmed spaces, one to find. Permits, sound, light, build, security, vendors.

Chapter 01 is a rave: budget goes to sound and light. Design Hub is meant to look like a warehouse. Any spend that makes it look expensive is spend against the brand.

**Owns:** venue contracts, permits, technical spec, suppliers, on-site safety, load-in and out.

### 03 — Partnerships
Season Partner, Chapter Partners, Experience Partners.

Sell roles, not logos. A partner owns a function inside the night — navigation, meeting, the after — and gets reported on in proof, not impressions.

**Owns:** pipeline, proposals, contracting, delivery against commitments, the Season One report in January.

### 04 — Passport and archive
The record layer. The thing that makes Season One worth something in 2027.

**Owns:** passport design and print, stamping at the door, the attendee list, the photo and film archive, the numbers that go in the January report.

## The passport, concretely

Paper. Card stock. Pocket sized. Ten boxes.

```
VILLE / KLA          No. ______

☐ Warehouse          ☐ New artist
☐ Courtyard          ☐ New place
☐ Hill               ☐ New neighbourhood
☐ Off the grid       ☐ Met someone new
☐ After dark         ☐ Made something
```

Four of the boxes are chapters and get stamped at the door. Six are missions and get stamped by asking — which means a conversation with every person who wants one, which is itself the point.

Track only two numbers this season: how many issued, and how many reach four or more stamps by 12 December. Those two numbers are the entire case for going digital in 2027.

---

## Weekly cadence

One standing meeting, 45 minutes, same day each week. Four items only:

1. **Roster** — what publishes this week, what's in production
2. **Production** — next chapter's critical path, anything slipping
3. **Partners** — pipeline movement, commitments due
4. **Numbers** — tickets sold, passports issued, spend against budget

Anything not in those four goes to a separate conversation. The meeting is for decisions, not updates.

---

## Decision gates

| Date | Gate | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|---|
| 26 Sep | Roster cast, first films shot | Campaign Phase One launches | Delay launch one week, not the chapter |
| 10 Oct | Season Partner signed | Chapter 01 at full budget | Chapter 01 at reduced budget, conversation moves to Chapter 02 |
| 24 Oct | Chapter 01 at 60% sold | Proceed | Open roster invite lists, no discounting |
| 15 Nov | Chapters 01–02 complete | Commit Chapter 03 and 04 spend | Consolidate to three chapters, protect the finale |
| 20 Dec | Season complete | Season Two planning opens January | Assess honestly before committing further |

Discounting is the one lever that is off the table. A ticket that drops in price tells everyone who already bought that they overpaid, and it breaks the same-room rule in a way people notice.

---

## Budget control

Production ladder: **15m → 15m → 22m → 30m**, total 82m.

Chapter 01 at 15m is not arbitrary. 300 tickets × 50,000 = 15,000,000. The night pays for itself at the door. That number is the floor of the whole season and shouldn't be raised to accommodate an idea, however good.

Chapters 03 and 04 spend more because by then there is proof behind the spend. If Chapters 01 and 02 underperform, Chapter 04's 30m is the first thing to cut — not the roster, not the archive, not the passport.

**Never cut:** the photographer, the filmmaker, the passport print. Those three are the entire asset the season produces.

---

## Language rules, 2027

In force from the moment anything goes public. These are not stylistic.

**Never use:** tournament names or abbreviations, the co-hosting bid name, CAF or confederation marks, the trophy, team kits, national team names, player likenesses, match schedules, or any phrasing implying official status, partnership or endorsement.

**Use instead:** the summer. The month the city fills up. Visitors. When the world comes to Kampala.

Rights are sold and enforced aggressively. One careless poster can cost the property more than a season earns.

Brief the roster on this in writing before they publish anything. They are the most likely source of an accidental breach, because they won't know the rule exists.

---

## What gets counted in January

The Season One report is the document that unlocks 2027. Build it from the start, not in December.

- Attendance per chapter, and repeat attendance across chapters
- Passports issued, and passports at four-plus stamps
- Roster reach, but reported alongside what it produced
- Work made: films, photo sets, collaborations formed, artists discovered
- Partner delivery against what was actually promised
- Financial: revenue, spend, margin per chapter

The honest version of this report is worth more than a flattering one. It is the thing a 2027 partner will read most carefully, and the first person to notice an inflated number will be the person holding the budget.

---

## The single biggest risk

Not money. Drift.

Four chapters in six weeks with a new property, a new roster and a partner pipeline running at the same time is a lot of surface area. The failure mode is that Chapter 01 goes well, everyone gets excited, scope grows, and Chapter 04 arrives underprepared in the most expensive week of the year.

The defence is the ladder and the gates above. Hold them.
