Ville

Same room. Different energy.

Ville is not an event. It's a town that moves.

Year One: eight stops between October 2026 and July 2027. Where each one goes, why that place, what it unlocks, what it leaves behind, and what a brand can do inside it. Partner document.

The idea

A ville is a town. VILLE is a town with no fixed address.

It doesn't run one event and repeat it. It moves — through places, communities and moments, carrying the same identity into each one. Some stops we build ourselves. Some we build with a partner who already holds the room. Some we enter, appearing inside a moment somebody else created.

The room changes every time. What travels is the rule, the roster, the archive and the passport. That's the property.

Which is why a brand doesn't sponsor a VILLE event. It enters the town. It takes a function inside a place people already want to be, and travels with it.

Stop 06

What makes a stop VILLE

Four things, in any place, whoever built it. One ticket, no VIP, no sections — nobody gets a better version of the night than anybody else. A room nobody has seen used that way before. The roster in it, making the record of it. And a passport marked at the door.

If a stop can't hold all four, it isn't a stop. It's an event we were at.

The names

Every stop is a Ville. The word in front says which energy.

Rave Ville is loud, dark and late. Switch Ville is three channels and a pair of headphones. Sunset Ville is fire, food and a view. Upstairs Ville is a rooftop with the city underneath it.

This is what makes the promise literal. Same room, different energy — the room is always VILLE, and the name is the energy. Somebody reading the calendar knows what kind of night each one is before they read a word of description, and a brand reading it knows which one it belongs in.

The rule: the word names the energy, never the address. Which is why the names travel. Rave Ville isn't the Design Hub — it's what VILLE does in a warehouse, anywhere. A rooftop in Nairobi is still Upstairs Ville.

So these are not four chapters. They are the town's four formats, and the road carries them. Which format lands at stops 05 to 08 is a live decision rather than a fixed one — with one strong candidate already: a silent disco is the only format that can be built inside somebody else's event without a single sound clash, which makes Switch Ville the natural shape for the guest stop.

Impact, built in

Two halves, and the passport records both. One is what a person takes out of a stop. The other is what the town leaves in the place.

Contribution
What the stop leaves behind

  • The archive given back to the people in it
  • One thing built that stays after we go

Why it's structural

A touring property that takes a crowd's money and leaves nothing is a tour. A town that arrives, hires locally, pays people who've never been paid for their work, and leaves two of its own seats behind is something a place invites back.

It is also the only version of impact a partner can report honestly — named people, paid briefs, a space handed back. Not a donation photographed at the end of the night.

Season One prints as designed: paper, ten boxes, discovery only. The contribution column joins the passport in the 2027 edition, once there's a road to record.

Year One

Eight stops, October 2026 to July 2027. The first four are the Kampala block — dated, spaced and budgeted. Everything after the January gate is designed but deliberately uncosted, because nothing on this road gets built on a forecast.

01

Rave Ville

The warehouse · Design Hub, Kampala · Saturday 31 October 2026

We build it
Where
A working industrial space in the middle of the city, run as a rave. Raw, dark, loud, late. No headliner, no lineup announced — sound and light are the whole production.
Why this place
It carries no social baggage. Nobody has a memory of who they're supposed to be in that room, which is the only way a first night sets its own standard instead of inheriting one.
What it unlocks
Kampala's creative core — the DJs, photographers, designers and filmmakers who decide what the city thinks is good. Get this room right and the other seven are invitations rather than pitches.
What we leave
The first 300 passports in circulation. The roster's first films. A technical spec and supplier list every later stop inherits — the town's first infrastructure.
What a brand gets
Founding position. The cheapest entry the town will ever sell, and the stop every later credit is measured against. A brand here isn't at the launch — it's in the origin story.

Date fixed · Space held · Late-event permit outstanding

02

Switch Ville

The courtyard · Tupe Hostel, UCU, Mukono · Saturday 14 November 2026

We build it with
Where
A university quadrangle, open air, run as a silent disco. Three channels, three DJs, everyone in the same courtyard listening to different things and switching between them. The cheapest ticket of the year at 25,000.
Why this place
The courtyard already has social memory — it is where these students already gather. VILLE inherits that rather than manufacturing it. It's also the first stop outside Kampala proper, which is the first proof the town travels at all. And the format fits the place: a silent disco puts no amplified sound into a residential hostel at night, so the neighbours and the permit stop being the reason this room can't happen.
What it unlocks
Students: the most commercially valuable audience of the year and the hardest to reach without being obvious about it. Reached here on their ground, at their price, by invitation of their own hostel.
What we leave
Paid briefs for student photographers and DJs on the night, credited by name. A campus route into the Season Two roster. The lowest door price of the year, so nobody in that room is priced out of their own courtyard.
What a brand gets
The youth room without the usual campus-activation awkwardness — no gazebo, no sampling table, no branded scaffold in a place people live. And for a connectivity brand the metaphor needs no explaining: three hundred people on headphones, choosing a channel, switching. The trade is stated openly: lowest ticket of the year, largest partner target of the block.

Date fixed · Space held · Host agreement to sign · Headphone hire for 300 to quote

03

Sunset Ville

The hill · Nkonzi Hill · Saturday 28 November 2026

We build it
Where
Sunset on open land. Fire, food off the grill, music that fits the light, and no building at all. The slow stop of the season — the one where people actually talk to each other instead of shouting over a system.
Why this place
It is the argument in physical form: VILLE doesn't need a venue, it needs a place. A town that can only exist where there's a licensed room isn't a town, it's a promoter.
What it unlocks
Everyone who would never queue outside a club — older creatives, the photographers who work in daylight, the people who bring their friends rather than arrive alone. A grill and a view do what a dancefloor can't: they make strangers sit down. It widens the town past the night crowd.
What we leave
The land exactly as found, to a stated pack-out standard, because the next hill depends on this one. The first landscape archive of the route. The address of Upstairs Ville revealed from the stage, to a room already in it.
What a brand gets
Daylight. The only stop that photographs in natural light — a golden hour that yields a brand's whole year of content in one afternoon, without a studio or a set.

Date fixed · Land held · Access, power and weather plan to confirm · Grill and food vendors to appoint

04

Upstairs Ville

The rooftop · address revealed late · Saturday 12 December 2026

We build it
Where
A rooftop. Balconies, the city underneath, and an address nobody gets until the week of. The name is the tease and the whole reveal: it tells you the night is up somewhere, and nothing else. Placed in the biggest week of the city's year. We don't reveal everything.
Why this place
December is when every brand in the country is already spending and every venue is already full. The finale doesn't compete with that calendar — it's the one room in it nobody can predict.
What it unlocks
Everyone the first three stops collected, in one place, plus everybody they dragged along. The first night the town is visibly a population rather than an audience.
What we leave
The season film. The only two numbers that matter: passports issued, and passports at four stamps. The January report, which is what the whole road is actually funded by.
What a brand gets
The end of a six-week story a partner was inside from the first frame — not a December activation bought in November. The report lands in January, when nobody else has anything to show.

Date fixed · Rooftop shortlist open · Capacity at height is the binding constraint · Address unannounced until December

Dec 20 – Jan

The town stops and counts. Season film cut. Passports counted. Season One report written honestly and sent to partners — attendance, repeat attendance, work made, collaborations formed, partner delivery against what was actually promised, money in and out per stop.

Stops 05 to 08 are committed off that report, not off this document. If the first four don't produce the proof, the road gets shorter, not louder.

05

The road stop

Jinja — Gulu as alternate · February 2027

We build it
Where
A town outside the capital with a scene of its own. Jinja is first choice: river, skate, boda and adventure culture, close enough to reach and far enough to count. Gulu is the alternate, and a stronger music case if the north opens first.
Why this place
This is the stop that tests the whole thesis. Nobody in that room has been to a VILLE. Nobody is there because of the Kampala crowd. If the identity holds here, the property travels; if it doesn't, better to learn it two hours away than across a border.
What it unlocks
A scene that currently has to send its people to Kampala to be seen. The town goes to them instead, which is a thing no cultural property in this market has bothered to do.
What we leave
Local crew, local sound, local photographers — hired, paid and credited. Two roster places for that town, held for Season Two. The first stop where the archive is made largely by people who don't live in Kampala.
What a brand gets
Presence upcountry that isn't a truck and a gazebo. For any brand whose distribution is national but whose marketing is Kampala, this is the stop that closes the gap — and the content from it doesn't look like a regional activation.

Proposal · Committed at the January gate · Space to secure

06

The guest stop

Inside somebody else's moment · March – April 2027

We enter it
Where
A moment that happens with or without us — a festival, a match weekend, a fashion show, a market. VILLE takes one space inside it and runs it as a stop of the town.
Why this place
It proves the town can appear inside existing culture instead of competing with it. We don't build the crowd, we build the room. It's the cheapest stop of the year and the fastest way to meet people who've never heard the name.
What it unlocks
An audience someone else assembled — including a football crowd, which arrives as a community rather than a broadcast, and is reachable here without touching anybody's rights.
What we leave
The host keeps the build and the content. Passports go out to people who didn't know the town existed that morning — the clearest measure of whether the identity carries on its own.
What a brand gets
A room of its own inside a moment where every other brand is buying signage. For a brand built on football communities, this is the entry point: the culture around the match, not the ninety minutes anyone has already sold.

07

Across the border

Nairobi or Kigali, with a local partner · May 2027

We build it with
Where
One city, once, built with people who already run rooms there. Not a tour — a stop.
Why this place
The property has to stop being Ugandan before the summer, not during it. Regional budgets are set at regional level, and a partner can't take VILLE to their regional board while it only exists in one city.
What it unlocks
A creative scene that already trades with Kampala's informally — the same photographers, DJs and designers who follow each other and have never been in a room together.
What we leave
A counterpart roster of twelve in that city, cast by the same rule. The first cross-border collaborations in the archive. A passport with two cities on it, which is the moment it stops being a ticket stub.
What a brand gets
Regional proof before the season everyone is planning for. One market becomes three, on evidence rather than a deck — and at the price of a single stop.

08

The summer

Kampala, daily · 19 June – 17 July 2027

The town at full size
Where
The whole city, for a month, while East Africa fills with visitors. Four rooms a day: morning, streets, after, tomorrow.
Why this place
It's the one moment nobody has to be convinced matters. Every brand in the region will build something around the ninety minutes inside the stadium. The other twenty-two and a half hours are unclaimed, and they belong to whoever already knows the city.
What it unlocks
Visitors who arrive asking where the city actually goes, and the entire population the previous seven stops assembled, in the same month.
What we leave
The routes, mapped by the people who live on them. A city guide made from a year of archive rather than a press trip. The passport as a visitor's route through Kampala — and the case for year two.
What a brand gets
First position on the cultural layer of the biggest moment East Africa has had in fifty years — bought at 2026 prices, before anyone else noticed it was for sale. Not an official anything. That's the point.

The rules that protect it

A moving property fails in predictable ways. These are the four that would kill it, and what stops each one.

Same room, same ticket

No VIP, no tables, no sections, at any stop, for anybody — partners included. The moment the town has a rope it becomes every other event in the city and the reason people came stops being true.

Nothing on a forecast

Stop 01 breaks even on tickets alone. Stops 05 to 08 are committed on the January report, not on this document. A brand saying no slows the road down; it never stops it.

Drift is the real risk

Not money. Eight stops with one roster and a live partner pipeline is a lot of surface area, and the failure mode is that stop 01 goes well, scope grows, and stop 04 arrives underprepared in the most expensive week of the year. The gates hold the line: fewer stops before worse ones.

The summer has language rules

VILLE will not use tournament names, marks, imagery or any wording implying affiliation, in any material, at any stop. Rights are sold and enforced. We say: the summer, the month the city fills up, visitors, when the world comes to Kampala. The roster is briefed in writing before it publishes anything.

What a partner is actually buying

Not four parties in Kampala, and not a promise about next summer. A property that will have visited eight places, hired in each of them and kept a record of all of it — with a position inside it that travels, reported in things a customer can verify.

The town happens either way. That's the only posture that produces a good deal, for both sides.
